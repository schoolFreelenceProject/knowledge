# IT Security Policy

## Password Rules

Employees must use strong, unique passwords for all company systems. Passwords
must contain at least 12 characters and include a mix of uppercase letters,
lowercase letters, numbers, or symbols. Passwords must not contain the employee's
name, company name, project name, birth date, or common words.

Multi-factor authentication is required for email, VPN, HR systems, finance
systems, source code repositories, customer platforms, and administrative tools.
Employees must not approve multi-factor authentication prompts they did not
initiate.

Passwords must not be shared with colleagues, managers, contractors, customers,
or support teams. IT will never ask for a password by email, chat, phone, or
ticket comment. Passwords must be stored only in the company-approved password
manager.

## Device Usage Policy

Company devices are provided for business use and must be protected from loss,
damage, unauthorized access, and malware. Employees must keep operating systems,
browsers, security tools, and business applications updated according to IT
instructions.

Personal devices may not be used to store company documents, customer data,
salary information, source code, credentials, or confidential business records.
Limited access from personal devices may be allowed only through approved
browser-based applications with multi-factor authentication and no local file
download.

Employees must lock their screen when leaving a device unattended. Lost or stolen
devices must be reported to IT Security immediately, ideally within one hour of
discovery. IT may remotely lock, wipe, or disable a device to protect company and
customer information.

## Data Protection Rules

Company data must be classified and handled according to sensitivity. Public
data may be shared externally after approval. Internal data is for employees and
approved contractors. Confidential data, including customer records, contracts,
financial data, security documents, and employee records, must be shared only
with authorized recipients.

Confidential data must be stored in approved company systems. Employees must not
upload confidential data to personal cloud storage, public AI tools, unapproved
file transfer services, or messaging accounts outside company control.

When sending confidential files, employees must verify recipients, use approved
sharing controls, and apply access expiration where available. Sensitive files
should not be attached to broad distribution lists. Data should be retained only
as long as required for business, legal, or compliance purposes.

## Security Incident Reporting Process

A security incident is any event that may affect confidentiality, integrity, or
availability of company or customer systems. Examples include phishing emails,
lost devices, suspected malware, unauthorized access, accidental data sharing,
credential exposure, or suspicious system behavior.

Employees must report suspected security incidents immediately through the IT
Security ticket queue or the emergency security hotline. Reports should include
the date and time, affected system, description of the event, people involved,
screenshots if safe to capture, and any actions already taken.

Employees must not delete evidence, forward suspicious emails to large groups,
contact suspected attackers, or attempt their own investigation. IT Security
will triage the incident, contain the risk, preserve evidence, coordinate with
Legal or HR when needed, and communicate required next steps.
